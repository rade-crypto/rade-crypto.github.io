# Ship a camera-first AI iPhone app

For a solo developer in Serbia who wants about $20k/month as fast as possible, the best-supported path in 2025–2026 is **a consumer iOS subscription app built around one AI "magic moment" from the camera, monetized with a hard paywall and grown through faceless short-video accounts**. Web SaaS, Chrome extensions and Claude/ChatGPT apps rank lower. My concrete pick is an AI skin-scan and skincare-routine coach. The evidence is lopsided. Verified tiny-team mobile apps in AI photo, astrology, makeup and calorie tracking sit at **$20k–$65k MRR**, and one of them reached about $50k MRR roughly four months after its iOS launch ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/category/mobile-apps); [TrustMRR Boothie](https://trustmrr.com/startup/boothie)). The web SaaS winners at that level mostly sell to audiences they built years earlier. The App Store also gets around Serbia's biggest structural problem. Stripe still does not serve Serbian businesses, but Apple and Google bill customers worldwide, handle consumer taxes and pay into a Serbian business FX account, at a 15% commission under their small-business programs. The odds are worse than the stories suggest. RevenueCat's 2025 data shows the **top 10% of apps grew MRR 306% while the bottom quartile shrank 33%**, and the median solo app earns under $1k/month. Expect $20k/month from a single first app within 12 months only in about 1 case in 10–20. With a disciplined "kill fast, ship the next one" loop, a realistic target is 12–24 months. A second constraint comes with success: **paušal taxation stops at 6M RSD a year (about €51k)**, which a $20k/month app passes in roughly three months, so you need a tax structure ready before the money arrives. Every source in this report is a published success story, which is survivorship bias by construction. The research tools could not load full pages, so every figure comes from search-engine snippets of the cited sources and needs re-checking before you rely on it.

## Revenue concentrates in camera-first consumer AI subscriptions

The clearest signal from 2025–2026 is that **consumer subscription apps with an AI "result screen" produce the largest new indie numbers**. RevenueCat's *State of Subscription Apps 2026* covers more than 115,000 apps and more than $16B in revenue ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). It shows AI apps converting trials to paid at **8.5% versus 5.6%** for non-AI apps and earning about 41% more per user. The catch is that they keep only **21% of subscribers after a year versus 31%**, and they refund more ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)). AI apps make money quickly and lose users quickly. That pushes the business model toward collecting money up front, through weekly plans, annual plans and hard paywalls, before churn sets in.

The case studies follow one template. Cal AI, a calorie tracker that works from a photo of your food, was built by teenagers. MyFitnessPal acquired it with a 7-person team after it passed **15M downloads and $30M+ in annual revenue in under two years** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3098039); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/myfitnesspal-cal-ai-acquisition)). Other sources put it at $40–50M, but $30M+ is the company-confirmed figure. Co-founder Blake Anderson had run the same playbook twice before: RizzGPT ($2.4M ARR) and Umax, an AI face-rating app ($5M ARR). He described it as "idea to execution 3x on this playbook in one year" ([Blake Anderson on X](https://x.com/blakeandersonw/status/1842349939088925146); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2024/10/07/hacking-the-app-store-gen-zs-15m-arr-bootstrapped-success-story/)). Below that outlier tier, the realistic band is visible on TrustMRR, which pulls revenue directly from RevenueCat and Stripe. Boothie, an AI photo editor, made $44k in 30 days, about four months after its June 2026 iOS launch. An AI astrology app shows $64k MRR, an anonymous AI makeup app $38k MRR, and TrackAI, a calorie tracker, **$20k MRR from 4,700 paying subscribers and 327k installs in 12 months** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/category/mobile-apps)). Faith apps offer a regional analogue. Bible Chat, built by a Romanian team, claimed $15M annualized revenue with more than 95% of it from the US ([Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/bible-chat-investment-round-faith-app-romania-feb-2025); [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20250418/amp?f=5)). Stella, an AI manifestation app launched in May 2026, reportedly crossed $300k MRR within two months ([BigGo/Starter Story](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/435ccd9f4d05042a)).

The pattern has four parts. The app does one job. It takes a camera or photo input. It shows a scored or shareable result that makes good short video. And it serves a recurring self-improvement or identity need, such as body, face, food, faith or fate. Inference cost is not the constraint. A single vision call costs cents, while AI wrapper gross margins run 25–60% only when heavy users or image generation are left uncapped ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/13/llm-api-cost-cogs-ai-wrapper-gross-margin-guide); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/ai-wrapper-business-model-monetization)). Marketing is the real cost line. Downloads alone do not pay either. A BookTok app with 200k downloads a month earned only $6k MRR ([Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/tiktoks-obsession-untapped-mrr)). Willingness to pay depends on the category's emotional stakes, not on reach.

## Web SaaS winners mostly sell to audiences they already own

The web side has bigger durable businesses but a worse path for someone starting from zero. The best-known indie earners are Pieter Levels and Marc Lou, and both rely on large X audiences. Levels' Photo AI is cited at $132–138K MRR at its late-2025 peak ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579)). One tracker reports it later falling toward ~$80K/month even as traffic rose ([StealWhatWorks](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/pieter-levels-still-making-money)). Marc Lou's portfolio made $83.7k in June 2026, led by TrustMRR at $30k and DataFast at $21k. His boilerplate ShipFast fell from **$17.2k in January to about $2–3k by mid-2026** ([Marc Lou on X](https://x.com/marclou/status/2071884698058698901); [Streakr](https://streakr.co/playbook/marc-lou)). AI coding agents now produce the scaffolding that boilerplates used to sell, so that category is fading (my inference). TypingMind, Tony Dinh's AI chat client, passed $1M in annual revenue within 20 months by moving buyers to team seats ([AIbase](https://news.aibase.com/news/14433); [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/typingmind)). That B2B shift is the clearest survival move in the category. Narrow utilities such as Tally (forms) and Senja (testimonials) reach millions a year with two or three people, but over several years ([flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/27-micro-saas-examples-that-actually-print-money-in-2025)).

Two forces work against a new web founder with no audience. First, **SEO, the classic no-sales-call channel, has weakened**. Seer Interactive measured organic CTR on queries with Google AI Overviews falling from 1.76% to 0.61% between mid-2024 and late 2025, with only a partial rebound since ([quickseo.ai](https://quickseo.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-statistics-2026-60-data-points-every-seo-should-know)). Google's March 2026 enforcement against scaled content hit thin programmatic-SEO sites ([madx.digital](https://www.madx.digital/learn/programmatic-seo-ai-overviews)). Smaller sites lost 60% of search referrals in two years ([xseek](https://www.xseek.io/blogs/articles/ai-traffic-decline-2026)). Second, generic AI wrappers keep getting absorbed by the model providers. Jasper's customers moved to ChatGPT ([DEV](https://dev.to/jamilxt/ai-wrappers-are-dying-three-business-models-survived-instead-5db4)), and model prices have collapsed ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/12/the-ai-wrapper-trap-when-your-moat-is-someone-elses-api-call)). The web products that survive own something: an audience, proprietary data, team workflow lock-in, or affiliates. HeadshotPro, for example, pays 30% recurring commission to affiliates ([revshare.so](https://www.revshare.so/programs/advertiser/headshotpro)).

Marketplaces are the strongest counterargument to going mobile. **The Chrome Web Store is one of the last places where search delivers free users.** Superpower ChatGPT went from a free extension built in three days to five-figure MRR, solo ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-a-free-chrome-extension-in-3-days-and-turning-it-into-a-5-figure-mrr-ecosystem-3rIbjigZxiFsrgqJjJYp)). Another solo developer reports about $10k MRR with 8% free-to-paid conversion, all from Web Store search ([iHeart](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/53-the-indie-hacker-podcast-wi-334456085/episode/how-a-solo-dev-hit-10k-338614911/)). The median extension tells a different story. TimeYourWeb has **20,000 users and $60 MRR after ten years** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-chrome-extension-in-2-weeks-10-years-later-20-000-users-10-paying-customers-60-mrr-YaK5THoaKqXgTlgBjHgO)). Extensions that ride ChatGPT or LinkedIn also live with the risk that the host builds the feature itself. The other marketplaces are side income. Paid Figma plugins with 500+ installs earn a median of $200–800/month, and niche Notion template sellers earn $800–2,500/month after six to nine months ([jakeinsight](https://jakeinsight.com/side-income/2026-03-12-create-and-sell-figma-plugins-as-a-developer/); [kupkaike](https://kupkaike.com/blog/is-selling-notion-templates-still-profitable-2026)). The Claude and ChatGPT directories that the earlier Shopify research looked at are a channel, not a business. OpenAI forbids selling subscriptions or digital goods inside ChatGPT apps ([Moburst](https://www.moburst.com/blog/chatgpt-app-directory-explained-what-brands-need-to-know/)), and estimates put monetized MCP servers below 5% ([mcpize](https://mcpize.com/blog/make-money-with-mcp)).

## Getting users without sales calls now means short video, store search or borrowed communities

The no-sales-call channel that actually produces mobile winners is **organic short video at volume**. Cal AI's early growth is attributed to plain screen-recording explainers with a voiceover ([Vidotoria](https://www.vidotoria.com/blog/how-to-market-app-without-showing-your-face)). POV Camera reportedly reached $100k+ MRR with networks of faceless TikTok slideshow accounts, and its founder admitted per-post engagement was low and that the result came from volume ([Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/pov-camera-app-faceless-tiktok-strategy-to-100k-mrr-200k-dl)). Dippy AI ran 60+ TikTok accounts to 60M views in three months ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/50-tiktok-accounts-ai-200-000-mrr-for-mobile-app-bd8f6f48ac)). The channel is crowding. TikTok carousel post volume grew 140% year over year while "almost every performance metric dropped" ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/tiktok-statistics-2026-marketing-data-points)). The edge now comes from hooks, niche angles and the number of accounts, not from the slideshow format itself.

Paid channels add to organic reach but cannot replace it at indie budgets. Apple Search Ads costs about **$1.58 per tap and $2.51 per install in the US** in 2026, and subscription apps pay $1–3.50 per tap ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/apple-ads-benchmarks-2026/); [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/apple-search-ads/research/apple-search-ads-costs/)). Human UGC creators charge **$100–500 per video**, and only about 5% of Meta creatives become winners ([Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-price); [Riffkit](https://riffkit.ai/blog/ugc-creator-rates-and-the-wrong-number)). The arithmetic follows from TrackAI's ratio: 327k installs produced $20k MRR. Buying that many US installs at $2.51 would cost about $820k, so **the installs have to come mostly from free video reach**. Paid search is for measuring conversion and catching people who are already searching. On the web, the equivalent first-customer channels are borrowed communities (34% of named channels in a 70-founder dataset, Reddit most often) and owned audiences ([lead-scorer](https://lead-scorer.com/blog/how-founders-get-first-customers-2026)). One founder reached $6.8k MRR on $20/day of Meta ads ([MRRStory](https://www.mrrstory.com/stories/this-indie-hacker-reached-6772-mrr-with-just-20day-meta-ads)).

Monetization design does as much work as distribution. Third-party readings of RevenueCat and Adapty data show weekly plans turning installs into trials at about **9.8% versus 1.8% for annual plans** ([Medium](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)). An unverified blog puts hard-paywall revenue per install at day 60 at $3.09, against $0.38 for freemium ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/hard-paywall-vs-free-trial-revenuecat-indie-app-2026)). Even if the gap is half that size, the order is clear: show the paywall right after the magic moment, and do not give the core result away.

## Serbia makes the App Store the cleanest rail, but paušal ends at about €51k

**Getting paid is the deciding constraint, and app stores solve it best.** Apple lists Serbia as a territory in its developer agreement and has run a Serbian storefront since 2020 ([Apple Exhibits](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/exhibits/Exhibits-to-Schedule-2-and-3-20250821-Croatian.pdf); [RATEL](https://ratel.rs/en/blog/apple-services-available-to-users-in-serbia)). Google Play added Serbian merchant accounts and RSD pricing ([SeeNews](https://seenews.com/news/google-adds-serbia-to-merchant-account-platform-671392); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2018/11/30/paid-apps-on-the-play-store-are-available-in-georgia-and-myanmar-local-currency-supported-in-paraguay-and-serbia/)). RevenueCat staff confirmed that both stores support Serbian developers ([RevenueCat Community](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/rn-payment-3978)). Apple needs a Paid Apps Agreement, bank details and a W-8BEN, and pays within 45 days of fiscal month-end ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/view-payments-and-proceeds/)). The **Small Business Program cuts the commission to 15%** on proceeds under $1M, but only after you apply ([Qonversion](https://qonversion.io/blog/apple-reduces-app-store-commission-to-15)), and Google Play offers 15% on the first $1M ([Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/app-developers-apple-google-small-business-programs/)). There is one warning sign. A 2026 forum thread reports a Serbian individual hitting an error on "Enroll today" with two accounts and getting no reply from support ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842061)). Enrolling as a registered business with a D-U-N-S number is the fallback (my inference). Do this in week one, not at launch.

Web billing is harder. **Stripe still does not support Serbia**, and the standard workaround is a US LLC or UK Ltd ([Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/how-to-open-a-stripe-account-in-serbia-with-a-us-llc); [Incorpuk](https://incorpuk.com/blog/how-to-open-stripe-account-in-serbia/)). That adds tax risk around place of management, which needs a Serbian adviser. Among merchants of record, **Polar explicitly lists Serbia** for payouts via Stripe Connect Express ([Polar docs](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/supported-countries)). Paddle (5% + $0.50) excludes only sanctioned countries, so it is likely usable but unconfirmed ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle); [Payhip](https://payhip.com/blog/dodo-payments-alternatives/)). Lemon Squeezy, Creem and Dodo were not confirmed for Serbia. Creem's local-bank list appears to omit Serbia, leaving a 2% USDC payout as its only route ([Creem](https://docs.creem.io/finance/payouts)). Whichever rail you use, a paušalac must receive foreign money into a **devizni account in the business's name** and convert it to RSD before withdrawing it ([Pausalko](https://pausalko.rs/blog/pausalac-i-devizni-racun-firme-sta-sve-treba-da-znate/)).

The tax ceiling is the surprise that comes with success. Foreign income counts toward the **6,000,000 RSD paušal cap** ([Pausalko 2026](https://pausalko.rs/blog/kako-pausalac-da-naplati-od-stranih-klijenata-2026/); [Biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/vesti/srbija/preduzetnicima-rok-do-kraja-oktobra-za-prelazak-na-pausalno-oporezivanje/)). A proposed rise to 8M is tied to EU accession and not enacted ([biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/vesti/srbija/privrednici-traze-povecanje-limita-za-pausalno-oporezivanje-na-osam-miliona-dinara/)). Crossing the cap forces a switch to bookkeeping-based taxation, reportedly with no grace period ([beancount.io](https://beancount.io/it/blog/2026/07/30/serbia-pausal-flat-rate-tax-freelancer-guide)). The VAT threshold is a separate 8M RSD of turnover ([biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/preduzetnik/porezi/pausalcima-se-nece-menjati-granica-za-ulazak-u-pdv/)). Paušal can carry the first several months, not the goal. Once the app passes about $3–4k/month, ask an accountant to plan the move to a knjigaš or a d.o.o., which can also deduct ad and creator spend as real expenses. Three questions need written answers from an accountant. First, whether Apple as the single payer creates independence-test exposure. That test targets employment-like contracting, not product sales to millions of consumers ([N1](https://n1info.rs/biznis/a533854-Nova-poreska-pravila-za-pausalce-problem-za-deo-IT-zajednice/)). Second, whether you owe reverse-charge VAT on Apple's commission and on Meta or Apple ad invoices. Third, how VAT applies to app sales through the stores. Serbian tax-practice sites have published opinions on that last question ([Neobilten](https://www.neobilten.com/tag/mesto-prometa/?lang=lat)). Serbia also has a cost advantage. Average net pay in programming is about **311k RSD/month (about €2,650)** ([Biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/vesti/srbija/prosecna-neto-plata-u-februaru-116-127-dinara/)), so an app earning $3–4k/month already beats a senior local salary, and you can keep going much longer than a US founder could. The Serbian precedents point to global English-language products. Clockify was bootstrapped from Novi Sad to $25M+ ARR after ten years of side projects ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/after-10-years-of-building-side-hustles-this-founder-built-a-25m-arr-success-GN8KEteJuEUDpmtjO6HA)), and Nordeus sold for up to $378M ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210602005247/en/Take-Two-Interactive-Software-to-Acquire-Mobile-Games-Developer-Nordeus)).

## The recommendation: an AI skin-scan coach for iPhone, sold on short video

**Build a single-purpose iOS app (Android later). The user takes a selfie, gets an AI "skin score" across about six visible traits (texture, redness, shine, dark circles, visible blemishes, evenness), a personalized AM/PM routine, and weekly progress photos that show the score moving.** "Glowscan" is a working name only; check trademarks and App Store names first. It combines the proven parts of the evidence. It has a camera magic moment and a shareable score, like Umax and Cal AI. It sits next to the verified $38k-MRR AI makeup app and the $50k AI photo editor ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/category/mobile-apps)). And unlike one-shot face rating, it has a weekly reason to come back, which directly addresses AI apps' 21% annual retention. A vision-model call per scan costs cents, so the margin goes to the store and marketing, not to inference. It avoids the most crowded clone category, Cal AI-style calorie trackers. Two risks are real. Present the app as cosmetic guidance, never as diagnosis: no "acne treatment," "rosacea" or medical claims, and keep a "see a dermatologist" disclaimer (my judgment based on App Store review and liability norms, not sourced). Big skincare brands and existing scanner apps compete in this space, and the research did not measure them. Spend part of week one on App Store searches to size them up. The fallback idea, if week-one validation fails, is a faith or astrology daily-reading app, which has the strongest willingness-to-pay evidence ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/hallow-lent-surge-prayer-app-revenue); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/category/mobile-apps)).

**Pricing and paywall** (my proposal, tuned to the weekly and hard-paywall data above): onboarding quiz, then the first scan, then the overall score with the trait breakdown blurred, then a hard paywall. Offer two plans: **$6.99/week with no trial**, and **$39.99/year with a 3-day free trial**, with the weekly plan shown as the anchor. Cap scans at a few per day to limit refund and abuse costs. Use RevenueCat or Superwall to A/B test the paywall from day one. After Apple's 15%, $20k/month net needs about **$23.5k gross**, which is roughly 900–1,200 active payers depending on the weekly/annual mix. TrackAI needed about 4,700 payers at a lower price point, so this target is ambitious but within the observed range.

**Growth channel and budget:** organic faceless short video is the primary engine. Run **5–8 TikTok/Instagram/YouTube Shorts accounts**, each with its own angle: before/after progress, "rating my skin routine," ingredient myths, reacting to the AI scoring celebrity-routine products. Post 2–3 times a day per account, using screen recordings of the scan as the core asset. Spend **about $6,000 over 12 weeks**. Put $2,500 into Apple Search Ads on exact-match intent terms in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, to measure tap → install → trial → paid. Put $2,500 into 10–15 micro-creator UGC videos at $150–250 with usage rights, then repost the winners and run them as Spark Ads. Keep $1,000 for AI UGC and editing tools, spare devices or SIMs, and incidentals. Stop paid spend on any channel where cost per paying subscriber exceeds about 40% of expected first-year revenue per payer.

**Kill criteria** (my thresholds). In week 1, if 30 test videos average under 1,000 views and none passes 10k, switch to the fallback idea before building. At launch plus 3 weeks, if install-to-paid is under 1.5% after the paywall tests, or 7-day refunds exceed 10%, rework the magic moment, and if a second attempt fails, kill. At week 12, **kill if MRR is under $1,000 and no account has produced a video over 100k views**. Keep going if MRR is $1–3k and growing more than 20% month over month. Double down if MRR is above $3k. When you kill an app, reuse the codebase (camera, scoring pipeline, paywall, account network) for the next idea within two weeks. Anderson's three apps in one year show that the system you build is worth more than any single app.

**Honest odds and timeline.** Only about 1 in 6 apps clears $1k/month, and the median solo app earns less ([Bug.hr citing RevenueCat](https://www.bug.hr/biznis/paywall-pretplate-poskupljenja-kako-zivjeti-od-softvera-48436); [You Could Ship This](https://youcouldshipthis.com/blog/indie-app-revenue-realistic-expectations)). My estimate is about a **5–10% chance that this first app reaches $20k/month within 12 months**. Treat that as a judgment, not a measurement. It rises to perhaps **20–30% over 18–24 months** if you run three or four kill-or-scale cycles. The fastest documented ramps (Boothie at about four months, Stella at two) are outliers from a self-selected leaderboard. TrackAI's 12 months to $20k MRR is the more realistic benchmark for a hit. The most likely good outcome in the first 12 weeks is a $500–3k MRR signal that tells you whether to scale this app or move on.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes what "fastest" means. For a no-audience solo founder, distribution is the scarce resource and payments are the hidden blocker, and consumer iOS is the only path that addresses both. Short video can deliver hundreds of thousands of installs without an audience or a sales call. Apple's billing replaces the Stripe account Serbia does not have. The web SaaS stories are real, but they are mostly returns on audiences built over years. Copying the product without the audience reproduces the TimeYourWeb outcome, not the Photo AI one.
