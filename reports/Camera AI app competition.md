# Skip skin scans, build the fit check

An AI skin-scan coach is no longer a good first bet for a solo developer in Serbia in late 2026. The generic "selfie → skin score → routine" app has dozens of 2024–2026 clones, free equivalents from Olay, La Roche-Posay, Neutrogena and Perfect Corp, saturated TikTok search pages, and a typical indie outcome of **$700–$1,300 MRR** ([TrustMRR Rash Scan](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rash-scan); [whatsthe.app SkinPal](https://www.whatsthe.app/skinpal-ai)). It also carries more legal and review risk than other camera niches: Apple 1.4.1 rejections for anything that reads as a health claim, and an Illinois biometric-privacy settlement of **$4.7M against Neutrogena Skin360** ([Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/open-lawsuit-settlements/4-7m-neutrogena-skin360-bipa-violation-class-action-settlement/)). The CEO's alternative, an AI outfit-rating "fit check" stylist, holds up. Outfit styling is the only "camera → AI result → paywall" niche in this research where apps launched in 2025–2026 show real money. Aesty has **$10k MRR verified through RevenueCat** ([whatsthe.app](https://www.whatsthe.app/aesty)). Stylio, launched in September 2025, is estimated (low confidence) at $88k–$470k a month ([AppCurrents](https://appcurrents.com/apps/stylio-personal-stylist-ios-6744065736)). Dressly draws about 28k downloads a month despite billing complaints ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/dressly-outfit-ai-stylist/world.dressly.fashion)). I confirm the niche and amend the wedge in two ways. First, market to **18–30, not 16–30**: press coverage tying appearance-rating apps to teen mental health makes minors a liability. Second, ship a unisex product and run a women's content account alongside the men's accounts in weeks 1–2. The paying apps in the evidence are women-skewed, and the young-men "fit check" market is a plausible bet but unmeasured. The evidence does not show acne tracking, men's skincare or the women's market as clearly better. The realistic outcome is a **5–10% chance of $20k/month within 12 months** and a likely $500–3k MRR signal by week 12. All figures here come from search-result snippets, not full page loads, and nearly every revenue number except the TrustMRR and RevenueCat-verified ones is a third-party estimate or a founder claim.

## Skin scanning is a red ocean where the median app earns about $1k a month

The skin category fails on three tests: competition, price floor and risk. On competition, the App Store is full of near-identical 2024–2026 apps: Skin Check, SkinBFF, MySkinAI, GlowScan, Bloom, Thea, Venus and others, mostly with fewer than 1,000 ratings ([GlowScan](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/glowscan-ai-skin-scanner/id6752527457); [Bloom](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bloom-ai-skin-scanner/id6754468117)). Established apps sit above them: Skan has ~9.8K ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skan-1-skincare-app/id6449196562)), and Lovi has 250K+ downloads plus seed funding ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/707843-89)). TikTok's discover pages for "skin scanner app" were full of competing apps and promo codes through October 2026 ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/skin-scanner-app)). On price floor, the core feature is free. Perfect Corp offers a free scan for "16 skin concerns," La Roche-Posay runs MyRoutine AI, and Haut.AI announced a generative "future skin" simulation for Olay in June 2026, which raises the bar for paid apps ([Perfect Corp](https://www.perfectcorp.com/business/showcase/skincare/home); [longevity.technology](https://longevity.technology/news/haut-ai-partners-with-olay-for-personalized-skin-simulation-tool/)). When a brand gives the result away to sell serum, an indie has to win on distribution alone.

The revenue spread follows a power law. At the top, one "GlowAI" skin app shows **$111k in 30 days, verified through RevenueCat**, in early 2026 ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/glow-ai-skin)). The 2024 Glow AI hit $10K MRR in two to three days after ~6M creator views paid partly in equity ([arr.club](https://www.arr.club/glow/glow-ai-mrr-at-10k-in-2-days)). The rest of the field looks like SkinPal AI: **$1,263 MRR, falling 7% a month, listed for sale at $13,000** ([whatsthe.app](https://www.whatsthe.app/skinpal-ai)). The breakouts came from paid creator campaigns, not better models. The risk test is the deciding factor. Developers report Apple 1.4.1 rejections even for "wellness-only" apps with disclaimers ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807508)), and wording like "may indicate [a condition]" turns an app into a medical-claim app ([drobinin](https://drobinin.com/consulting/healthkit-medical/building-medical-and-healthkit-apps-on-ios/)). The Kenvue settlement matters more for a selfie app. In March 2025 the court held that Skin360 is not medical, so it gets no healthcare exemption, and BIPA sets statutory damages at **$1,000 per negligent and $5,000 per intentional violation** ([Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/open-lawsuit-settlements/4-7m-neutrogena-skin360-bipa-violation-class-action-settlement/)). An app whose core input is a close-up of the face carries that exposure on every scan.

The two skin sub-niches I tested do not change the verdict. The acne tracker is the strongest of them. New 2026 entrants such as Acne Tracker: Skin Journal and AntiAcne have tiny rating counts, and a progress timeline gives a real reason to subscribe ([Acne Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/acne-tracker-skin-journal/id6770239249); [AntiAcne](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/antiacne-your-acne-journey/id6765994413)). But acne sits closest to the 1.4.1 line ("acne grade" reads as medical), it still requires face photos, and breakout close-ups are poor share material for faceless video. Men's skincare looks like white space only because a search turned up no dedicated app ([OnSkin roundup](https://onskin.com/6-beauty-apps-you-should-use-in-2026/)). An empty search result is weak evidence, and the niche carries the same face-photo and BIPA burden. Neither is clearly better than the fit check. The acne tracker is a reasonable second app once the camera-to-paywall pipeline exists.

Across ten other camera niches, money pools where incumbents lock it up. Plant identifiers took **78% of a ~$27M/month identifier market** in May 2025, mostly through PictureThis with about 1.8M reviews ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20250509?f=1); [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/plant-identification-apps-ranked-picturethis-plantin-plantnet-2026)). Coin and rock ID belong to Next Vision and clone studios. Homework solvers belong to ByteDance and Google ([FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/bytedances-gauth-ai-study-companion-dominates-2025-us-education-charts-as-top-ai-homework-helper/)). Room redesign is full of failed indies, including Decorify AI with **$13.85 in all-time revenue** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/decorify-ai-interior-design.md)). AI pet care shows ~$7 MRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-pet-care-health)). Styling is the exception: several fresh apps make money at different scales, and no studio dominates.

## Confirm the fit check, but rate the outfit, not the boy

The CEO's core logic holds. The niche has verified money and no lock-in. It analyzes images rather than generating them, so it is cheap to run. The output is a score that makes good short video. And the clearest documented complaint against the visible leader, Dressly's weekly billing and cancellation friction, gives "transparent pricing" a concrete target ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/dressly-outfit-ai-stylist/world.dressly.fashion)). The same billing complaints dominate reviews of PictureThis, Skan and AI Dermatologist ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/plant-identification-apps-ranked-picturethis-plantin-plantnet-2026); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/skan-ai-skincare-and-beauty/6449196562); [Trustpilot](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/ai-derm.com)), so an anti-trap stance works as marketing across camera apps generally.

Two parts of the plan need correction. The first is the age floor. Fortune's 2024 coverage of looksmaxxing apps that "rate teen boys' faces" and later press on worsening teen mental health show where reputational and App Review risk concentrates ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/01/looksmaxxing-apps-rate-teen-boys-faces-mental-health/); [texxr](https://texxr.com/868607/looksmaxxing-apps-worsen-teen-mental-health)). A product that scores 16-year-olds invites the same story. The fix costs almost nothing. Target ads and content at 18–30. Score only clothing, fit, color and proportion, never the face, body or attractiveness. Write the rule into the model prompt and the App Store copy. That one rule also separates the app from the looksmaxxing apps it will be compared to.

The second is the women's market, which the evidence does not settle either way. The apps with proven revenue (Stylio, framed as "helping women"; Aesty; Dressly) appear women-skewed, so women's styling is the proven market. It is also the contested one. The young-men wedge rests on two reasonable inferences: Umax showed young men paying $3.99/week for appearance judgment, and "fit check" culture is male-coded and underserved by the current stylist apps. But no source in this research measures demand or revenue for a men's outfit-rating app. That is not a reason to switch to women. Leading with men avoids fighting three funded incumbents for the same audience, and a mirror fit pic hides the face behind the phone, which suits faceless content. It is a reason to build the product unisex from day one and let the week 1–2 content test decide. If the women's account's hooks beat the men's accounts by 2x or more on average views, change the wedge before launch.

## Product, pricing and why inference is not the cost that matters

The product should differentiate on four things competitors either lack or do badly. **First, repeatable scores.** The most damaging complaint about Umax and LooksMax AI is "submitted the same picture 3 times, got a different number" ([realworldappeal](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/looksmaxxing-apps-compared)). Use a fixed rubric (fit, color harmony, proportion, occasion match, cohesion, each 1–10), temperature zero, and a cache keyed on an image hash so the same photo always returns the same score. **Second, fixes from the user's own closet.** Each result should name two or three specific changes ("swap the white sneakers for your brown boots, roll the cuff once") using items the user has photographed, not shopping links. **Third, an occasion mode** (date, interview, wedding guest, gym-to-street), which gives users a repeat reason to scan before every event. **Fourth, transparent billing as a visible feature.** The paywall should show the annualized cost of the weekly plan, the app should send a reminder before any trial converts, and cancellation instructions should be one tap from settings. Leave virtual try-on and image generation out of version one. Generation costs far more per result than analysis and would force credit caps ([roomcalm](https://roomcalm.com/blog/best-ai-room-design-tools)).

For the paywall, keep the template that converts. The onboarding quiz (style, occasions, budget) leads to the first scan, then the overall score with the fixes blurred, then a hard paywall. Weekly plans turn installs into trials at about **9.8% versus 1.8% for annual plans** ([Medium, RevenueCat/Adapty data](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)). So transparency here means honest framing, not dropping the weekly plan. I propose **$5.99/week with no trial, $12.99/month, and $39.99/year with a 3-day trial and a day-2 reminder**. That undercuts Dressly's $11.99/week by half and stays above Umax's $3.99. After Apple's 15% Small Business rate, $20k net requires about **$23.5k gross**. At Aesty's observed ~$14.60 per subscriber, that is roughly **1,600 active payers**, or about 2,000–2,400 with a more annual-heavy mix. For scale, TrackAI needed 4,700 payers and 327k installs over 12 months to reach $20k MRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/category/mobile-apps)). An install-to-paid rate around 1.5–2% implies on the order of 80k–150k installs before the payer base reaches that size, and almost all of them must come from free video reach. Buying them through Apple Search Ads at about **$2.51 per US install** would cost $200k or more ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/apple-ads-benchmarks-2026/)).

Inference cost barely affects the decision, as long as the app analyzes images rather than generates them. Vision calls cost cents or less per image, and AI-wrapper margins collapse only when heavy users or generation go uncapped ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/13/llm-api-cost-cogs-ai-wrapper-gross-margin-guide); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/ai-wrapper-business-model-monetization)). My working estimate, not sourced in the notes, is **$0.005–0.02 per scan** on a mid-tier vision model with a compressed image and a short structured output. At 1,600 payers scanning about 30 times a month, that comes to roughly $250–1,000 a month, or 1–4% of gross. The one free blurred scan per install adds about $500–3,000 across 150k installs. Two controls keep it there: a daily cap of about 10 scans per subscriber to block abuse, and resizing images on the device before upload. Marketing and Apple's commission remain the real cost lines.

## Privacy by design: neck-down capture and zero retention

Outfit rating has a structural privacy advantage over skin scanning: **the face is not needed at all.** Build the privacy approach on that. The capture guide frames the shot from the neck down, and mirror fit pics usually hide the face behind the phone anyway. If a face does appear, crop or blur it on the device before upload. Do not compute, store or send any facial landmarks, embeddings or identifiers. Whether on-device face detection used only to blur counts as a "scan of face geometry" under BIPA is a legal question I could not resolve from the notes. The safest default is neck-down capture with no face detection, plus an optional manual blur tool. On the server, process each photo and delete it immediately, and use a model API tier that does not retain or train on inputs (check the provider's terms). Wardrobe items the user saves should be cropped garment images stored on the device, with an optional sync that contains no person in the frame.

Add a short consent screen before the first capture. It should say the app analyzes clothing only, photos are deleted after scoring, and no biometric data is collected. AntiAcne already markets "photo discarded after scan" as a feature ([AntiAcne](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/antiacne-your-acne-journey/id6765994413)), and the same line belongs in the fit check's App Store listing. This design cuts the Kenvue-style exposure to near zero rather than merely managing it: there is no facial geometry to consent to, store or leak ([Global Cosmetics News](https://www.globalcosmeticsnews.com/jj-settles-biometric-privacy-suit-over-neutrogena-skin360-app/)). It also keeps the app far from Apple 1.4.1, because nothing about clothing is a health measurement. Apple 4.3 (near-duplicate apps) and 4.2 (thin AI wrappers) are the relevant review risks ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812655); [ptkd](https://ptkd.com/journal/app-store-rejection-4-3-a-ai-slop-design-spam)). The closet-aware fixes, occasion mode and repeatable rubric are the distinct workflow to describe in review notes.

## A 12-week plan, a content engine and hard kill lines

The Serbia-specific setup from the earlier research still applies. Enroll in the Apple Developer Program in week one, as a registered preduzetnik with a D-U-N-S number if individual enrollment errors, as one Serbian developer reported in 2026 ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842061)). Apply for the 15% Small Business Program, since it is not automatic ([Qonversion](https://qonversion.io/blog/apple-reduces-app-store-commission-to-15)). Receive payouts into a devizni business account ([Pausalko](https://pausalko.rs/blog/pausalac-i-devizni-racun-firme-sta-sve-treba-da-znate/)). Plan the move off paušal before revenue crosses the **6M RSD (~€51k) annual cap**, which a $20k/month app reaches in about three months ([Biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/vesti/srbija/preduzetnicima-rok-do-kraja-oktobra-za-prelazak-na-pausalno-oporezivanje/)).

| Week | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 | Apple enrollment, Paid Apps Agreement, W-8BEN, devizni account, Small Business Program application; book the accountant. Create 4 accounts (2 men's streetwear, 1 men's smart-casual, 1 women's) on TikTok, IG and Shorts. Post 15 mocked "AI rates this fit" videos made with a prototype prompt in a notebook. |
| 2 | Post 15 more videos (30 total). **Content gate:** compare men's and women's hook performance and pick the wedge. Write the scoring rubric; test repeatability on 50 photos (target: same photo, same score; ±0.5 across reshoots). |
| 3 | SwiftUI scaffold: neck-down capture guide, on-device resize, scoring API with zero-retention handling, result screen. RevenueCat or Superwall integrated. |
| 4 | Closet: photograph and tag up to 30 garments; fixes reference owned items. Onboarding quiz. Blurred-fixes hard paywall at $5.99/wk, $12.99/mo, $39.99/yr. |
| 5 | Occasion mode, shareable score card with watermark, trial-reminder notification, one-tap cancel help. Privacy policy and consent screen. Scale to 6 accounts based on what the hooks show. |
| 6 | TestFlight with 40–60 testers recruited from video comments. Fix repeatability and any fix that suggests items the user doesn't own. |
| 7 | App Store submission, with review notes explaining the distinct workflow (against 4.3/4.2). Commission the first 5 micro-creator fit-check videos. |
| 8 | **Launch** in US, UK, CA and AU. Link in bio everywhere. Apple Search Ads on exact-match terms ("outfit rater," "fit check app," "what to wear") at ~$30/day. |
| 9 | Paywall A/B tests on price, trial versus no trial, and copy. Track install → paywall view → paid daily. |
| 10 | Add a weekly "fit streak" and a style-progress history (retention). Repost winning creator videos as Spark Ads. Commission 5–10 more creator videos. |
| 11 | Second paywall test round. Start Android only if iOS install-to-paid is ≥2%. Start d.o.o./knjigaš paperwork if MRR is above $3k. |
| 12 | Decision review against the kill criteria. |

**The content plan** runs 6–8 faceless accounts, 2–3 posts a day each, all built on screen recordings of the scan. The formats with the strongest fit for the niche are: "rating your fits 1–10" mirror shots with the phone covering the face; "fix this fit using only what's in my closet" flat-lays and hanger shots; "same jeans, three fits, watch the score climb" sequences; "AI roasts my fit" with a harsh-but-funny score; and occasion prompts ("first-date fit, 6.8 → 8.9 with one swap"). Accounts split by angle: men's streetwear, men's smart-casual and date-night, gym-to-street, a roast account, and one or two women's accounts kept as a live test of the wedge. Use only fits that followers submit with permission. Do not score celebrities. The budget follows the earlier plan, about **$6,000 over 12 weeks**: $2,000 for Apple Search Ads to measure conversion, $2,500 for 10–15 micro fit-check creators at $150–250 a video with usage rights, $1,000 for editing and AI tools and spare devices, and $500 in reserve. Human UGC costs $100–500 a video, and only about 5% of ad creatives become winners ([Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-price); [Riffkit](https://riffkit.ai/blog/ugc-creator-rates-and-the-wrong-number)). Treat creators as hook research and repost the winners on owned accounts. The channel is crowding: TikTok carousel volume rose 140% year over year while engagement fell ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/tiktok-statistics-2026-marketing-data-points)). That means the gains come from the number of accounts and the sharpness of hooks, not from the format.

**Kill criteria** (my thresholds) work at three gates. At week 2, if 30 videos average under 1,000 views and none passes 10k, pivot to the fallback (a specialized coin or collectible identifier) before writing app code. At launch plus three weeks, if install-to-paid stays under 1.5% after two paywall tests, or 7-day refunds exceed 10%, rework the result screen once and kill on a second miss. Two 4.3 rejections in a row mean reposition, not resubmit. At week 12, **kill if MRR is under $1,000 and no video has passed 100k views**. Continue if MRR is $1–3k and growing more than 20% a month. Double down above $3k. A killed app still leaves reusable assets: the capture, scoring and paywall pipeline and the account network move straight into the next idea, which is the acne tracker or the coin identifier.

**Honest odds.** Only about 1 app in 6 clears $1k a month, and the median solo app earns less ([Bug.hr citing RevenueCat](https://www.bug.hr/biznis/paywall-pretplate-poskupljenja-kako-zivjeti-od-softvera-48436)). A 2026 benchmark puts a typical solo founder at 12–24 months to reach $3k MRR ([IndieRadar](https://indieradar.app/blog/fake-mrr-pandemic-real-benchmarks)). My judgment is a **5–10% chance that this app reaches $20k/month within 12 months**, about the same as the skin-scan app's odds but with much lower legal downside and a less crowded field. The odds rise to perhaps **20–30% over 18–24 months** across three or four kill-or-scale cycles. The most likely week-12 result is $500–3k MRR. That is enough to tell you whether to scale, and in Serbia it already approaches a senior developer's net salary of about €2,650 ([Biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/vesti/srbija/prosecna-neto-plata-u-februaru-116-127-dinara/)). Every estimate here carries real error. Third-party revenue models miss by 5–25% even by their vendors' own account, and one developer saw Sensor Tower show $40K/mo against actual revenue of $90K ([Appaloma](https://www.appaloma.com/research/app-intelligence-commodity-2026); [mirava](https://www.mirava.io/blog/how-to-check-app-revenue)). Stylio's $88k–470k range is the shakiest figure in this report.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from which camera app has the biggest market to which one a solo developer can enter without fighting brands, studios or lawyers. Skin scanning loses on all three counts. The fit check wins mainly on what it leaves out: no face, no health claims, no image-generation bill, and no incumbent with a million reviews. Its real differentiators, repeatable scores and fixes drawn from the user's own closet, also answer the most common complaints in the category.

The one open bet is the audience. The women's styling market has the proven revenue, and the young-men market has the open space. Neither the CEO nor this report can know which converts better without data, and two weeks of mocked videos, costing almost nothing, will show which audience responds before any code is written. Run that test first, keep the product out of under-18 appearance scoring, and treat the first app as the start of a reusable pipeline rather than a one-time bet.
