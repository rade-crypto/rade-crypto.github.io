# Day-count arithmetic for Daniel 8:14 and 12:11-12

Julian dates carry about +-1 month uncertainty (lunar months / intercalation); conclusions hold across that range.

- altar desecrated ('abomination'), 15 Kislev SE 145 (1 Macc 1:54): ~6 Dec 167 BCE
- first pagan sacrifice, 25 Kislev SE 145 (1 Macc 1:59): ~16 Dec 167 BCE
- Temple rededicated, 25 Kislev SE 148 (1 Macc 4:52): ~14 Dec 164 BCE
- death of Antiochus IV reported in Babylon, Kislev SE 148 (BM 35603): ~1 Dec 164 BCE

Actual interval desecration -> rededication: 1104 days; first sacrifice -> rededication: 1094 days (1 Macc 4:54: 'at the very season and on the very day' - exactly three years).

| Daniel's figure | counted from | lands on (approx.) | what happened then |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,150 days (2,300 evening+morning offerings, 8:14) | backward from rededication | 21 Oct 167 BCE | plausible start of the suppression of the tamid (autumn 167); fits if the count began before the altar desecration |
| 2,300 days (if 'evening-morning' = a full day) | backward from rededication | 28 Aug 170 BCE | c. 171: murder of Onias III (2 Macc 4:33-35) - a possible but different reading |
| 1,290 days (12:11) | from desecration | 18 Jun 163 BCE | nothing recorded; Antiochus already dead 199 days, Temple restored 186 days earlier |
| 1,335 days (12:12) | from desecration | 2 Aug 163 BCE | nothing recorded |
| 3.5 'times' (7:25, 12:7) ~ 1,260-1,280 days | from desecration | 19 May 163 BCE - 8 Jun 163 BCE | nothing recorded |

Reading: the three different figures (1,150 / 1,290 / 1,335) all exceed the real 3-year interval, and the two later ones fall in 163 BCE when nothing corresponding happened. A writer who already knew the date of the rededication (Dec 164) and of Antiochus' death would be expected to give the real interval; the successive, lengthening figures look like expectations recalculated before the outcome was known.
