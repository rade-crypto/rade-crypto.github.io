# The historical frame of Daniel 1-6, 9-11 checked against Babylonian and Persian records

Primary records referred to: Babylonian Chronicles ABC 5 (Nebuchadnezzar's early years) and ABC 7 (Nabonidus Chronicle); the Verse Account of Nabonidus; the Nabonidus cylinder from Ur; the Harran stelae of Nabonidus; the Cyrus Cylinder; dated Babylonian contract tablets; the Behistun inscription; Herodotus 3.89; Ptolemy's Canon. Biblical comparison texts: Jeremiah, Ezekiel, 2 Kings, 2 Chronicles, Ezra-Nehemiah.

| Daniel says | The record | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Belshazzar ruled Babylon when it fell (Dan 5, 7:1, 8:1) | Bēl-šarra-uṣur, eldest son of Nabonidus, ran Babylon while his father spent ten years at Teima (Nabonidus Chronicle years 7, 9-11; Verse Account: "he entrusted the kingship to him"; oaths sworn by Nabonidus and Belshazzar together). Herodotus, Xenophon and Berossus do not name him. | **Correct and notable**: preserves a genuinely Babylonian memory lost to the Greek historians. |
| Daniel is made "third ruler in the kingdom" (5:7, 16, 29) | Fits a realm with Nabonidus first and Belshazzar second. | **Consistent** (reading "third" as a rank title is also possible). |
| Belshazzar is "king" and the "son" of Nebuchadnezzar (5:2, 11, 13, 18, 22) | He never bore the title king (šarru) and the New Year festival lapsed in his father's absence; his father Nabonidus was a usurper, not of Nebuchadnezzar's line. | **Inaccurate** (unless "king" is loose usage and "son" means "successor"). |
| Babylon fell to "Darius the Mede," son of Ahasuerus, aged 62, who "received the kingdom" and was "made king over the realm of the Chaldeans" (5:31, 6:1, 9:1, 11:1); Cyrus came after him (6:29) | Babylon was taken by Cyrus' army under Ugbaru on 16 Tashritu 539 (Nabonidus Chronicle iii); tablets are dated to Cyrus as "king of Babylon, king of the lands" from the weeks after the conquest; no Median king intervenes. Media had already fallen to Cyrus in 550 (Chronicle ii 1-4). Darius I (522-486) was a Persian, son of Hystaspes and father of Ahasuerus/Xerxes. | **No match**. The sequence Babylon -> Media -> Persia does not fit the record; the names look like a conflation of later Persian kings. |
| Darius set 120 satraps over the kingdom (6:2) | Behistun lists 23 lands; Herodotus 3.89 counts 20 satrapies. | **Does not match** (cf. Esther 1:1, 127 provinces). |
| Nebuchadnezzar's court is staffed by satraps, ʾădargāzrayyāʾ, gədābrayyāʾ, dətābrayyāʾ, tiptāyēʾ (3:2-3) | These are Old Persian titles of the Achaemenid administration (after 539). | **Anachronistic** for Nebuchadnezzar (605-562); natural for a writer under Persian or later rule. |
| Nebuchadnezzar besieged Jerusalem in Jehoiakim's 3rd year (606/605) and took vessels and captives (1:1-2) | ABC 5 records Carchemish and the conquest of "Hatti" in 605 and tribute from "all the kings of Hatti" in 604, but no siege of Jerusalem. Jeremiah 25:1 and 46:2 make Jehoiakim's 4th year Nebuchadnezzar's 1st (Carchemish); Jer 36:9, 29 (Jehoiakim's 5th year) still treat Babylon as a future threat. Dan 1:1-2 shares wording with 2 Kgs 24:1 ("became his servant three years") and 2 Chr 36:6-7 (vessels placed in his temple in Babylon). | **Not supported** by the contemporary sources; looks derived from Kings/Chronicles. |
| Nebuchadnezzar lived seven "times" as a beast and was restored (ch. 4) | No Babylonian record. Nabonidus' ten-year absence at Teima is documented (Chronicle; Harran stele). The Qumran Prayer of Nabonidus (4Q242, see dss text) has Nabonidus struck "with an evil disease... in Teima" for seven years and helped by a Jewish diviner (gzr) "from the exiles," with the same list of idols of silver, gold, bronze, iron, wood, stone and clay as Dan 5:4, 23. | **Probably a tradition about Nabonidus** transferred to Nebuchadnezzar. |
| Nebuchadnezzar boasts "Is not this great Babylon which I have built?" (4:27) | His own building inscriptions and Berossus (in Josephus, Against Apion 1.139-141) stress exactly this. | **Consistent** (but the tradition was still known c. 280 BCE). |
| Four Persian kings in all (11:2) | Ten (or thirteen) kings from Cyrus to Darius III; the Hebrew Bible names exactly four: Cyrus, Darius, Ahasuerus, Artaxerxes. | **Compressed**: the Persian period is known only in outline. |
| "Chaldeans" = a guild of diviners (2:2-10, 4:4, 5:7, 5:11) | In Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Kings and Babylonian usage of the 6th century "Chaldean" is an ethnic/dynastic term (Daniel itself also uses it so at 5:30 and 9:1). The professional sense appears in Greek writers from Herodotus (1.181-183) on. | **Later usage.** |
| "Nebuchadnezzar" spelt with -n- (32 of 32) | Jeremiah (29 of 37) and Ezekiel (4 of 4) use the -r- form closer to Akkadian Nabû-kudurri-uṣur; Kings, Chronicles, Ezra, Esther use -n-. | **Matches the later writers.** |
| Daniel the wise hero (1-6) | Ezekiel 14:14, 20; 28:3 cites a righteous sage "Dn'l" (consonants דנאל, not דניאל) beside Noah and Job, as a figure of the distant past. Names Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, Azariah all recur in the lists of Ezra 8:2 and Nehemiah 8:4, 10:3, 10:7, 10:24. | Suggestive of a legendary name and of names current in the 5th century. |

## Tally
- Early, accurate knowledge: Belshazzar's role, "third ruler," the building boast.
- Inaccurate or anachronistic: the 605 siege, Belshazzar as Nebuchadnezzar's son and king, Darius the Mede and a Median kingdom after Babylon, 120 satraps, Persian titles at Nebuchadnezzar's court, the madness story (Nabonidus tradition), four Persian kings, the professional "Chaldeans," the -n- spelling.
