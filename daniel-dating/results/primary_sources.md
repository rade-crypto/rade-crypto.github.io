# Ancient sources used to check Daniel 8 and 11

Fetched and read from online translations: Polybius (Paton, Loeb), Diodorus and Strabo (Loeb) via LacusCurtius; Appian (White), Livy (Roberts), Josephus (Whiston) and Herodotus (Godley) via Perseus; Justin (Watson) and Cosmas (McCrindle) via tertullian.org; 1-2 Maccabees (World English Bible); the Canopus and Raphia decrees via attalus.org; the Babylonian king lists and chronicles via livius.org (van der Spek's translations). Modern margin dates in translations were ignored. Julian conversions of Babylonian months are computed (new moons + 19-year cycle), about ±1 day.

## Persia (Dan 11:2)
- Herodotus 7.20-21: Xerxes spent four years equipping his force; "For what nation did Xerxes not lead from Asia against Hellas?" 7.27-29 (Pythius: "the richest man we know of after you").
- Ptolemy's Canon: 10 Persian kings from Cyrus to Darius III (Cyrus, Cambyses, Darius I, Xerxes, Artaxerxes I, Darius II, Artaxerxes II, Artaxerxes III, Arses, Darius III); not counted are Bardiya, Xerxes II and Sogdianus. Daniel 11:2 has four.

## Alexander and the partition (Dan 8:5-8, 11:3-4)
- Diodorus 19.105.1-4: Cassander has Roxane and Alexander IV murdered (311/310); "there being no longer anyone to inherit the realm, each... held the territory... as if it were a kingdom won by the spear."
- Diodorus 20.28.1-3: Heracles, son of Alexander and Barsine, murdered (309/308).
- Diodorus 20.53.2-4: Antigonus, Demetrius, Ptolemy, Seleucus, Lysimachus and Cassander take the royal title (306/305); 21.1.4b: the coalition of "four kings" at Ipsus (301).

## Ptolemies and Seleucids (Dan 11:5-20)
- Appian, Syr. 65: Antiochus II "had two wives, Laodice and Berenice... a daughter pledged to him by Ptolemy Philadelphus. Laodice assassinated him and afterward Berenice and her child. Ptolemy, the son of Philadelphus, avenged these crimes... He invaded Syria and advanced as far as Babylon." (Justin 27.1 blames Seleucus II.)
- Babylonian King List BM 35603 obv. 12-13: news of Antiochus II's death, Abu SE 66 (Aug 246). Chronicle BCHP 11: Ptolemy III in Babylonia, Kislimu 66 - Shabatu 66 (Dec 246 - Feb 245).
- Adulis inscription (OGIS 54, in Cosmas): Ptolemy III "collected all the spoils of the temples which had been taken away from Egypt by the Persians" and brought them to Egypt. Canopus decree (7 Mar 238): "he recaptured the images and brought them back to Egypt."
- Polybius 5.79-87; Raphia decree: battle of Raphia, 10 Pachons year 5 of Ptolemy IV = 22 June 217; Antiochus defeated; Ptolemy grants a truce.
- Polybius 16.18.2 ("the siege of Gaza and the engagement between Antiochus and Scopas at the Panium"); 16.39.3-4 and Josephus, Ant. 12.129-137: after Panium the Jews receive Antiochus III and help besiege the Ptolemaic garrison in Jerusalem's citadel.
- Livy 35.13.4; Appian, Syr. 5; Josephus, Ant. 12.154: Antiochus III gives his daughter Cleopatra to Ptolemy V (winter 194/193 or 193/192).
- Livy 33.19-20; Appian, Syr. 1: campaigns along the coasts of Cilicia, Lycia, Caria and into Thrace (197-196).
- Polybius 21.17, 21.42-43; Livy 37.38-45, 38.38: Magnesia (190 BCE, L. Cornelius Scipio) and the Peace of Apamea (188 BCE).
- Diodorus 28.3, 29.15; Strabo 16.1.18; Justin 32.2; BM 35603 rev. 6-7: Antiochus III killed while attacking a temple in Elymais (Simanu SE 125 = June/July 187).
- 2 Maccabees 3: Seleucus IV sends Heliodorus to seize the Temple treasury. Appian, Syr. 45: "Seleucus was assassinated as the result of a conspiracy of a certain Heliodorus." BM 35603 rev. 9: Seleucus IV died 10 Ululu SE 137 (c. 3 Sept 175).

## Antiochus IV (Dan 8:9-14, 8:23-25, 11:21-45)
- Appian, Syr. 45: Heliodorus driven out by Eumenes and Attalus "who installed Antiochus"; the heir Demetrius was a hostage in Rome (Polybius 31.2). BM 35603: Antiochus IV and his co-regent son; the son killed by Antiochus' order, Abu SE 142 (Aug 170).
- 2 Maccabees 4: Jason buys the high priesthood (175), Menelaus outbids him (c. 172/1); Onias III lured from sanctuary at Daphne and killed by Andronicus (c. 171/170). (Josephus' accounts differ.)
- First Egyptian campaign: Polybius 28.18-21 (Pelusium; Eulaeus' counsel), Diodorus 30.14-18, Livy 44.19, 45.11; 1 Macc 1:16-19. Temple plundered on the return, SE 143 = 169 (1 Macc 1:20-24; 2 Macc 5:11-21 places it after the second campaign).
- Second campaign, the Day of Eleusis (July 168): Polybius 29.27 ("he drew a circle round Antiochus and told him he must remain inside this circle until he gave his decision... he led his army back to Syria, deeply hurt and complaining"); Livy 45.12; Diodorus 31.2; Appian, Syr. 66.
- 1 Macc 1:54, 59: "On the fifteenth day of Chislev, in the one hundred forty fifth year, they built an abomination of desolation upon the altar"; sacrifice on the 25th. 1 Macc 4:52-54: rededication 25 Chislev SE 148. 2 Macc 6:2: Temple renamed for Olympian Zeus. Babylonian-era conversion: Dec 167 and Dec 164.
- Character: Polybius 26.1a ("Epimanes"); 26.1.10-11 (lavish gifts and honours to the gods surpassing his predecessors); 30.25-26 (Daphne festival funded by plunder of Egypt and temples); Livy 41.20.9 (temple of Jupiter Capitolinus at Antioch); 1 Macc 3:30.
- Death: Polybius 31.9 ("on his retreat he died at Tabae in Persia, smitten with madness, as some people say"); 1 Macc 3:31-37 and 6:1-16 (eastern campaign from SE 147; dies in Persia/Babylon, SE 149); 2 Macc 9 (Persepolis/Ecbatana); 2 Macc 1:13-16 (temple of Nanaea); Appian, Syr. 66 ("robbed the temple of Venus Elymais; then died of a wasting disease"); Josephus, Ant. 12.354-359. BM 35603 rev. 14: "Year 148, Kislimu: It was heard that Antiochus (IV), the king, [died]" = c. 20 Nov - 18 Dec 164 BCE.
- No source records a third Egyptian campaign after 168 or a death in Judea "between the sea and the holy mountain."

## Not verified online
The Gurob papyrus; the astronomical diaries (ORACC unreachable); the exact date of Berenice's marriage (c. 252) and of Panium (c. 200); which Seleucid era 1 Maccabees uses.
