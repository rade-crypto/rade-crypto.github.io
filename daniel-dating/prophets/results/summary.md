# Best case of a fulfilled prophecy written before the event: Hebrew prophets

Method: every verse in Isaiah-Malachi (+ Dan 9:24-27) making a future statement about a named place, people or
person (1,141 verses, results/inventory_*.csv) -> 51 testable claims (scripts/claims.py) -> checked against
Dead Sea Scroll coverage (results/claims_evidence.csv), manuscript dates (sources/manuscript_dates.*),
Assyrian/Babylonian records (sources/cuneiform_events.*) and later non-biblical writers (sources/classical_events.*).
Scores are judgements (scripts/score.py, results/ranking.csv): proof of prior writing, outside record,
specificity, plain-reading match, unexpectedness, independence.

Top case: Isaiah 7:16; 8:1-4 (Damascus and Samaria despoiled by the king of Assyria within ~1-2 years), matched
by Tiglath-pileser III's inscriptions (RINAP 1: Tigl. III 20, 21, 42, 47, 49; Eponym Chronicle 733-732).
Limit: no manuscript older than 732 exists for any prophet; the outcome was a plausible forecast.

Pattern: the claims that can be PROVED older than the event by a dated scroll (Babylon's eventual abandonment,
Hos 3:4, Jer 30:11, the modern return) are generic, only partly matched, or influenced by the texts themselves;
the precise, well-matched claims (Isa 8, Isa 20, Amos, Hosea, Nahum) rest on internal evidence for their date.

Base rate: 'desolate forever' predictions about named foreign cities: 3 came true in the long run
(Nineveh, Babylon, Memphis), 9 did not (results/desolation_base_rate.csv).

Correction (Cyrus, Isa 44:28-45:1): an earlier draft dated these chapters to Cyrus's lifetime because they address
exiles in the present tense. That is not valid evidence: Hebrew prophecy routinely describes future events as
present or past (e.g. Amos 5:2 'fallen is virgin Israel', decades before 722). The evidence available - the book
heading (Isa 1:1), Ben Sira 48:24-25 (c. 190 BCE) and the Great Isaiah Scroll (one continuous book) - presents it
as Isaiah's; language measurement cannot separate Isa 40-55 from Isa 1-39. Its date is undecided by the data;
under the heading's date it ties Isa 8:1-4 for first place (score 12), under a 540s date it scores 10.

Re-scoring of the dispersion/return prophecies (D03, D04) after a full look: they had been under-scored.
Return (Isa 11:11-12; Jer 16:14-15; Ezek 36:24; 37:21-22; Amos 9:14-15): P3 E3 S2 M2 U2 I0 = 12, tied first,
and the ONLY top case whose prior writing is physically proven (scrolls ~2,000 years before 1948).
Weaknesses: the returnees knew and were moved by these texts; accompanying details (one Davidic king,
Philistia/Edom/Moab, drying the sea of Egypt) have not happened. Survival (Jer 30:11; 31:35-37): 11.

Second correction (independence): scoring the return prophecies I=0 treated the whole outcome as if it were up to
people who knew the texts. Only the WISH to return and to stay distinct was theirs. The scattering (Assyria, Babylon,
Rome), the loss of temple and king (Rome, 70/135 CE), the expulsions that drove most immigration, and the events that
made return possible were the acts of outsiders not acting on these texts. Re-scored I=0.5: return 12.5 (first),
survival 11.5; Hos 3:4-5 re-scored 11.
