# Cuneiform evidence for events relevant to the prophetic books

Primary texts only: editions/translations of the ancient sources plus catalogue data. No modern interpretive literature is summarized. Brackets `[ ]` = restored text, `( )` = translator's additions, `...` = illegible/omitted, exactly as in the edition quoted. Machine-readable version: `cuneiform_events.csv` (same folder).

**Editions used**
- RINAP 1-5 (Tadmor/Yamada/Novotny/Frame/Grayson/Leichty/Jeffers), RIBo, SAAo (SAA 18, SAAS 2), ADsD (ADART 1) on ORACC (CC BY-SA). Texts were read from the ORACC HTML pages (`https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/<project>/<Q/P/X-id>/html`); ORACC's JSON zips hold catalogue + lemmatized transliteration, but not the English translations.
- Babylonian Chronicles: livius.org translations adapted from Grayson, *Assyrian and Babylonian Chronicles* (TCS 5, 1975) and Glassner (2004); livius's bracketed year glosses such as "[598/597]" and its notes are omitted in the quotes below. Where livius differs from or omits something, the older translation of A. L. Oppenheim in Pritchard (ed.), *ANET* (3rd ed. 1969; pp. 304-308, 563-564) is quoted as a check (archive.org user upload `Pritchard1950ANET_201608`, a reprint of the 3rd ed.; standard ANET page numbers; OCR text, so fractions are uncertain where noted).
- Catalogue data: CDLI (cdli.earth), ORACC catalogues, eBL.

## 3. Sennacherib 701 (RINAP 3)

Rassam Cylinder (Sennacherib 4; BM 22503 etc.; dated Ayyāru, eponymy of Mitūnu = 700) lines 42-58; same text with minor variants in Chicago/Taylor Prism (Sennacherib 22; dated 691/689) ii 73-iii 49, Jerusalem Prism (23), Cyl. C (15), King Prism (17), 18, 140, 142, 165.
- **Eltekeh** (Rassam 42-45): "They formed a confederation with the kings of Egypt (and) the archers, chariots, (and) horses of the king of the land Meluḫḫa, forces without number, and they came to their aid. In the plain of the city Eltekeh, they sharpened their weapons while drawing up in battleline before me. ... I fought with them and defeated them. In the thick of battle, **I captured alive the Egyptian charioteers (and) princes (lit. "the sons of the kings"), together with the charioteers of the king of the land Meluḫḫa.**" Translit. 45: `LÚ.EN GIŠ.GIGIR.MEŠ ù DUMU.MEŠ LUGAL.MEŠ KUR.mu-ṣu-ra-a-a a-di LÚ.EN GIŠ.GIGIR.MEŠ ša LUGAL KUR.me-luḫ-ḫa bal-ṭu-su-un ... ik-šu-da ŠU.II-a-a`.
- **46 cities** (49-51): "(As for) Hezekiah of the land Judah, I surrounded (and) conquered forty-six of his fortified walled cities and small(er) settlements in their environs, which were without number, by having ramps trodden down and battering rams brought up, the assault of foot soldiers, sapping, breaching, and siege engines. I brought out of them 200,150 people, young (and) old, male and female, horses, mules, donkeys, camels, oxen, and sheep and goats, which were without number, and I counted (them) as booty."
- **Jerusalem** (52-54): "As for him (Hezekiah), I confined him inside the city Jerusalem, his royal city, like a bird in a cage. **I set up blockades against him and made him dread exiting his city gate.** I detached from his land the cities of his that I had plundered and I gave (them) to Mitinti, the king of the city Ashdod, and Padî, the king of the city Ekron, (and) Ṣilli-Bēl, the king of the land Gaza, (and thereby) made his land smaller." Translit. 52: `šá-a-šú GIM MUŠEN qu-up-pi qé-reb URU.ur-sa-li-im-ma URU LUGAL-ti-šú e-sír-šu URU.ḪAL.ṢU.MEŠ UGU-šú ú-rak-kis-ma a-ṣe-e KÁ.GAL URU-šú ú-ter-ra ik-ki-bu-uš` (*ḫalṣū/birātu* "fortresses, outposts"; "made exit from his city gate a taboo for him").
- **Tribute afterwards** (55-58): "As for him, Hezekiah, fear of my lordly brilliance overwhelmed him and, after my (departure), he had the auxiliary forces (and) his elite troops whom he had brought inside to strengthen the city Jerusalem ... (along with) 30 talents of gold, 800 talents of silver, choice antimony ... together with his daughters, his palace women, male singers, (and) female singers brought into Nineveh, my capital city, and he sent a mounted messenger of his to me to deliver (this) payment and to do obeisance." (`EGIR-ia ú-še-bi-lam-ma`)
- Other Judah statements: Nebi Yunus (34) 15: "I ruined the wide district of the land Judah (and) imposed my yoke on Hezekiah, its king." Smith Bulls 2-3 (44) 20b: "I ruined the wide district of the recalcitrant (and) strong land Judah (and) I made Hezekiah, its king, bow down at my feet." Lachish relief epigraph (66, BM 124911): "Sennacherib, king of the world, king of Assyria, sat in (his) armchair and the booty of the city Lachish passed before him." Azekah text (1015, K 6205 + 82-3-23,131) 5'-8' (city Azaqâ "between my ... and the land Judah", taken "by having r]amps [trodden down], mighty battering rams brought up").
- **What the texts do NOT say:** A lemma search of all RINAP 3 texts naming Jerusalem/Hezekiah (Senn. 4, 15-19, 22, 23, 26, 34, 42, 44, 46, 140, 142, 143, 165) finds no claim that Sennacherib entered, captured, or plundered Jerusalem, and no siege ramp (*arammu*) against Jerusalem: ramps and battering rams are attributed to the 46 cities (and Azekah); for Jerusalem only confinement, "blockades/outposts" and the later delivery of tribute to Nineveh are claimed. Hezekiah is not said to be deposed, captured or deported.

## 4. Murder of Sennacherib, 681/680

**ABC 1 iii 34-38** (livius): "On the twentieth day of the month Tebêtu, Sennacherib, king of Assyria, was killed by his son in a rebellion. For twenty-four years Sennacherib ruled Assyria. After the twentieth day of the month Tebêtu the rebellion continued in Assyria until the second day of the month Addaru. On the eighteenth day of the month Addaru Esarhaddon, his son, ascended the throne in Assyria." *Not stated:* the son is not named. [livius ABC 1.iii](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-1-from-nabu-nasir-to-samas-suma-ukin/abc-1.iii/)

**Esarhaddon, Nineveh A** (Esarhaddon 1; prisms dated 673/672) i 23-ii 11 (excerpts): "Persecution (and) jealousy fell over my brothers and they forsook (the will) of the gods ... They alienated the well-meaning heart of my father from me ... Afterwards, my brothers went out of their minds ... they plotted evil, girt (their) weapons, and in Nineveh, without the gods, they butted each other like kids for (the right to) exercise kingship ... I did not wait for my army ... I was not afraid of the snow (and) cold of Šabāṭu (XI) ... before my (arrival) in the territory of the land Ḫanigalbat all of their crack troops blocked my advance ... those rebels ... fled to an unknown land ... In Addaru (XII), a favorable month, on the eighth day ... I joyfully entered Nineveh, my capital city, and I sat happily on the throne of my father ... I sought out every one of the guilty soldiers, who wrongly incited my brothers to exercise kingship over Assyria, and imposed a grievous punishment on them: I exterminated their offspring." *Not stated:* Nineveh A never says Sennacherib was killed, nor names any brother. ## 6. Esarhaddon: Marduk's 70 years reversed to 11 (RINAP 4)

Esarhaddon 104 (Babylon A; prisms BM 78223 etc., 674-673) i 34-ii 9: "The Enlil of [the go]ds, the god Marduk, became angry and plotted evilly to level the land (and) to destroy its people. ## 7. Fall of Nineveh 612; Harran 610-609 (ABC 3 = BM 21901, CDLI P555783)

livius (after Grayson): **Year 12 (614):** "the Medes ... captured Tarbisu ... encamped against Aššur. They did battle against the city and destroyed it ... The king of Akkad and Cyaxares met one another by the city and together they made an entente cordiale." **Year 14 (612):** "The king of Akkad mustered his army and marched to Assyria. The king of the Medes marched towards the king of Akkad and they met one another at [...]-u. The king of Akkad and his army crossed the Tigris; Cyaxares had to cross the Radanu, and they marched along the bank of the Tigris. In the month Simanu, the Nth day, they encamped against Nineveh. From the month Simanu until the month Âbu - for three months - they subjected the city to a heavy siege. On the Nth day of the month Âbu they inflicted a major defeat upon a great people. At that time Sin-šar-iškun, king of Assyria, died. They carried off the vast booty of the city and the temple and turned the city into a ruin heap The [lacuna] of Assyria escaped from the enemy and, to safe his life, seized the feet of the king of Akkad. On the twentieth day of the month Ulûlu Cyaxares and his army went home." Then "Aššur-uballit ascended to the throne in Harran to rule Assyria." **Year 16 (610/609):** "the Medes ... marched to Harran against Aššur-uballit ... Fear of the enemy overcame Aššur-uballit and the army of Egypt that had come to help him, and they abandoned the city, and crossed the Euphrates. The king of Akkad reached Harran, fought a battle, and captured the city. He carried off the vast booty of the city and the temple." **Year 17 (609):** "Aššur-uballit, king of Assyria, with a large army from Egypt crossed the river Euphrates and marched against Harran ... Until the month Ulûlu they did battle against the city but achieved nothing." [livius ABC 3](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-3-fall-of-nineveh-chronicle/)
**Akkadian terms** (Oppenheim, ANET 304-305): "[Cyaxar]es, the king of the Manda-hordes (*Umman-manda*) marched towards the king of Akkad"; Cyaxares written *U-ma-ki-iš-tar* (note 10: "[*U-ma-kiš*]-*tar*"); "From the month Simanu till the month Abu, three ba[ttles were fought, then] they made a great attack against the city ... On that day, Sinsharishkun, king of Assy[ria fled to] ... The city [they turned] into ruin-hills and hea[ps (of debris)]." *Note:* the clause on Sîn-šar-iškun is damaged; livius/Grayson has "died", ANET restores "fled". The chronicle does not mention flooding or a river breach.

## 8. ABC 5 (BM 21946, CDLI P555785): Carchemish, Ashkelon, Arabs, Jerusalem

livius: **Obv. 1-8 (605):** "In the twenty-first year the king of Akkad stayed in his own land, Nebuchadnezzar his eldest son, the crown-prince, mustered the Babylonian army ... marched to Karchemiš which is on the bank of the Euphrates, and crossed the river to go against the Egyptian army which lay in Karchemiš. They fought with each other and the Egyptian army withdrew before him. He accomplished their defeat and beat them to non-existence. As for the rest of the Egyptian army which had escaped from the defeat ... in the district of Hamath the Babylonian troops overtook and defeated them so that not a single man escaped to his own country. At that time Nebuchadnezzar conquered the whole area of Hamath." **Obv. 15-20 (604):** "In the first year of Nebuchadnezzar in the month of Simanu he mustered his army and went to the Hatti-territory ... All the kings of the Hatti-land came before him and he received their heavy tribute. He marched to the city of Aškelon and captured it in the month of Kislîmu. He captured its king and plundered it and carried off spoil from it. He turned the city into a mound and heaps of ruins and then in the month of Šabatu he marched back to Babylon." **Rev. 9'-10' (599/598):** "In the sixth year in the month of Kislîmu the king of Akkad mustered his army and marched to the Hatti-land. From the Hatti-land he sent out his companies, and scouring the desert they took much plunder from the Arabs, their possessions, animals and gods. In the month of Addaru the king returned to his own land." **Rev. 11'-13' (597):** "In the seventh year, the month of Kislîmu, the king of Akkad mustered his troops, marched to the Hatti-land, and besieged the city of Judah and on the second day of the month of Addaru he seized the city and captured the king. He appointed there a king of his own choice, received its heavy tribute and sent to Babylon." [livius ABC 5](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-5-jerusalem-chronicle/)
ANET 564 (Oppenheim, from Wiseman 1956, BM 21946 rev. 5-15): "laid siege to the city of Judah (*Ia-a-hu-du*) and the king took the city on the second day of the month Addaru. He appointed in it a (new) king of his liking, took heavy booty from it and brought it into Babylon." *Not stated:* Jerusalem, Jehoiachin, Zedekiah and the number of deportees are not named; "city of Judah" = URU *Ia-a-hu-du*. Rev. 6'-7' (601): battle with Egypt in which "they smote the breast of each other and inflicted great havoc on each other. The king of Akkad turned back".

## 11. Nabonidus Chronicle (ABC 7 = BM 35382, Sp. II 964, CDLI P388185) and Cyrus Cylinder
