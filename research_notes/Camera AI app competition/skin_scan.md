# AI Skin-Scan / Skincare-Routine Apps: Competitive Landscape (as of Oct 2026)

> Method note: WebFetch and direct App Store / iTunes lookup were blocked in this environment (DNS / proxy 403), so all data below comes from search-result snippets of App Store pages, trackers (TrustMRR, screensdesign, trendapps, Apptopia), press and founder interviews. Ratings counts are snapshots from search indexes and may lag the live store by weeks. **No category-rank data (Health & Fitness / Lifestyle / Beauty) could be verified**, so it's listed under Gaps.

## Which apps exist, and how big are they (ratings, pricing, revenue, team)?

### Takeaway
The category is crowded and commoditized. There is a long tail of near-identical "AI Skin Scanner" selfie apps launched 2024–2026, a few breakout consumer apps (Skan, Glow-type apps, Lovi, Umax for face-rating), medical-grade players with CE certification (SkinVision, Skinive), and B2B/brand incumbents (Haut.AI powering Olay, Perfect Corp, Neutrogena Skin360, La Roche-Posay). Verified revenue is rare. The best data point: a "GlowAI" skin app showing ~$111k revenue in 30 days, RevenueCat-verified on TrustMRR (snapshot May 2026). The long tail mostly earns $0.7k–$5k/mo.

### Inferences
- Revenue in this category is power-law distributed. A handful of apps with strong distribution reach $30K–$100K+/mo, while most clones sit around $1K/mo (SkinPal's $1.3K MRR and $13K asking price, Rash Scan's ~$700) or below.
- The core feature (selfie → scores → routine) is free from brands and B2B vendors, so it gives no defensibility. Winners compete on distribution and paywall/onboarding conversion, not model quality.

## What do 1–3 star reviews complain about, and what gaps could a new entrant exploit?

### Takeaway
The top complaints are aggressive paywalls and weekly auto-renew billing traps, inconsistent or random scores (the same photo gets a different score), product scanners that misidentify items, and generic routines. A trust-first app (transparent pricing, repeatable scores, on-device or discard-photo privacy) and vertical niches (acne progress tracking, men, body acne, non-English markets, ingredient scanning for specific conditions) are the visible openings.

### Cited Findings
- Skan reviews: paywall and pricing are the most frequent negatives, and "the ai scanner barely works… would never register the correct product." — [mwm.ai Skan](https://mwm.ai/apps/skan-ai-skincare-and-beauty/6449196562)
- AI Dermatologist: Trustpilot complaints about unauthorized charges ($7.99 + $14.99) and refunds not received. — [Trustpilot](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/ai-derm.com)
- Umax / LooksMax AI: users report "submitted the same picture 3 times, got a different number." — [realworldappeal](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/looksmaxxing-apps-compared)
- Psychologists criticize looksmaxxing apps for worsening teen mental health (Fortune, Jul 2024). That is a reputational and App Review risk for face-rating apps. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/01/looksmaxxing-apps-rate-teen-boys-faces-mental-health/)
- Accuracy claims are unvalidated (e.g. MySkinAI advertises "~95% accuracy"). — [MySkinAI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6748348557)
- Privacy is a differentiator. AntiAcne advertises that the face photo is discarded after the scan. — [AntiAcne](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/antiacne-your-acne-journey/id6765994413)
- Acne-tracking apps are appearing in 2026 (Acne Tracker: Skin Journal, aimed at adult/hormonal/body acne, plus AntiAcne and AcneTrack's food-trigger scanner). The niche is emerging but not yet dominated. — [Acne Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/acne-tracker-skin-journal/id6770239249); [AcneTrack](https://acne-track.com/)
- I found no app explicitly marketed as men's skincare scan in 2026, apart from looksmaxxing apps. — (search null result; see [OnSkin roundup](https://onskin.com/6-beauty-apps-you-should-use-in-2026/))
- Korean-language entrants exist (e.g. "글로우 – AI 스킨케어 루틴"), so local-language markets are being contested too. — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/%EA%B8%80%EB%A1%9C%EC%9A%B0-ai-%EC%8A%A4%ED%82%A8%EC%BC%80%EC%96%B4-%EB%A3%A8%ED%8B%B4/id6738579245)

### Inferences
- Exploitable gaps: (1) **longitudinal tracking** (consistent lighting/pose capture guidance plus a progress timeline) rather than one-off scores, which fixes the "random score" complaint; (2) **men's skincare/grooming** framed around routine and acne rather than attractiveness rating, avoiding the looksmaxxing backlash; (3) **condition-adjacent journaling** (acne, rosacea, eczema flare logs, tret/isotretinoin purge tracking) without diagnosis claims; (4) **ingredient compatibility** with a user's own routine (OnSkin and Lovi own generic ingredient scanning); (5) **non-English markets** (LatAm Spanish/Portuguese, DE, PL, Balkan languages) where clone density is likely lower (unverified).

## Regulatory and platform risk (Apple 1.4.1, FDA, EU MDR, biometric privacy)

### Takeaway
Cosmetic "skin score and routine" apps are low regulatory risk if they avoid diagnosis language. Anything that detects or names a condition (melanoma, rosacea, eczema, "acne grade" as medical) invites Apple 1.4.1 rejection without clearance, and EU MDR device classification (SkinVision needed Class IIa). FDA's Jan 2026 wellness guidance loosened rules for wellness products. Illinois BIPA is the sleeper risk: Neutrogena Skin360 settled for $4.7M over facial-geometry consent.

### Cited Findings
- Apple guideline 1.4.1: apps must disclose data and methodology for health-measurement accuracy claims, and Apple will reject if accuracy can't be validated. Medical apps with clearance should submit documentation. — [conductatlas mirror](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/medical-app-accuracy-and-regulatory-clearance-requirements/)
- Developers report 1.4.1 rejections even for apps framed as "wellness-only" with disclaimers. Apple kept asking for regulatory approval. — [Apple Dev Forums 807508](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807508); [772732](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772732)
- Wording like "may indicate [a condition]" turns an app into a medical-claim app in Apple's eyes. — [drobinin](https://drobinin.com/consulting/healthkit-medical/building-medical-and-healthkit-apps-on-ios/)
- FDA, 6 Jan 2026: updated General Wellness and CDS guidances expand enforcement discretion for non-diagnostic wellness products, issued without public comment. — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102meea/digital-health-policy-fda-relaxes-restrictions-over-wearables-and-ai-decision-ma/); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/01/fda-cuts-red-tape-on-clinical-decision-support-software)
- No skin-cancer assessment app has US regulatory approval (date of report unclear). SkinVision showed 80% sensitivity / 78% specificity in small studies. — [Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/smartphone-apps-fall-short-on-skin-cancer-diagnosis/)
- SkinVision obtained EU MDR Class IIa on 5 Aug 2025, which shows that skin-cancer detection is a regulated medical device in the EU. — [Business Reporter](https://www.business-reporter.co.uk/healthcare/from-wellness-to-medical-device-skinvision-raises-the-bar-for-ai-in-healthcare)
- BIPA: Kenvue (Neutrogena Skin360) agreed to a $4.7M settlement (Melzer v. Kenvue, D.N.J.). In March 2025 the court held Skin360 is non-medical, so it gets no healthcare exemption. Statutory damages are $1,000 per negligent and $5,000 per intentional violation. A reported Charlotte Tilbury $2.9M settlement is unverified. — [Top Class Actions, 9 Oct 2026](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/open-lawsuit-settlements/4-7m-neutrogena-skin360-bipa-violation-class-action-settlement/); [Global Cosmetics News](https://www.globalcosmeticsnews.com/jj-settles-biometric-privacy-suit-over-neutrogena-skin360-app/)

### Inferences
- Safe zone for a solo developer: a cosmetic/wellness framing ("skin hydration appearance", "routine consistency"), no disease names in outputs or App Store metadata, explicit written BIPA-style consent before capture, on-device processing or immediate photo deletion, and no facial-geometry retention. A "mole check" or "rash identification" feature means medical-device territory and should be avoided.

### Gaps
- No public App Review rejection case specific to a cosmetic skin-scan app was found. The 1.4.1 evidence comes from other health categories.
- I didn't research EU AI Act obligations for biometric categorization in cosmetic apps.

## Verdict: crowded or open, and which sub-niche is winnable?

### Takeaway
**The generic category is crowded and red-ocean.** "Selfie → skin score → routine" has dozens of 2024–2026 clones, free brand equivalents (Olay/Haut.AI, La Roche-Posay, Perfect Corp, Neutrogena), and creator-saturated TikTok. A solo developer can still make money, but only with a distribution edge or a sharp niche. The realistic indie outcome is $1–10K MRR. Breakouts ($30K–$100K+/mo) depend on viral creator campaigns, not product quality.

### Cited Findings
- Indie floor: SkinPal AI at $1,263 MRR and declining, listed for sale at $13K; Rash Scan at ~$700/mo. — [whatsthe.app](https://www.whatsthe.app/skinpal-ai); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rash-scan)
- Ceiling for a well-marketed new app: GlowAI at $111k in 30 days (verified, early 2026); Glow AI at $10K MRR in 2–3 days (2024); Umax at $170K–700K/mo (2024). — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/glow-ai-skin); [arr.club](https://www.arr.club/glow/glow-ai-mrr-at-10k-in-2-days); [huxiu](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4050088.html)
- Incumbents are moving up-market with generative "future skin" simulations (Haut.AI SkinGPT for Olay, Jun 2026), raising the bar for generic scan apps. — [longevity.technology](https://longevity.technology/news/haut-ai-partners-with-olay-for-personalized-skin-simulation-tool/)

### Inferences
- **Most winnable sub-niches (ranked):**
  1. **Acne / breakout progress tracker** (adult, hormonal, body acne, tretinoin/Accutane journeys): has a retention-driven subscription logic, an emerging but unconsolidated field (2026 entrants have tiny rating counts), and a clear wedge against "random score" complaints through consistent capture and timeline comparison. It must stay non-diagnostic.
  2. **Men's skincare routine coach** without attractiveness rating: there's an apparent white space, and it can borrow looksmaxxing distribution (male TikTok) while avoiding its mental-health backlash.
  3. **Localized non-English scan/routine apps** (e.g. Balkan/CEE, LatAm, DACH): lower clone density likely, but unverified. Lower ARPU.
  4. **Routine-aware ingredient conflict checker** (scans products against *your* stack, e.g. retinol + acids): this differs from OnSkin/Lovi's generic "toxicity" scanning.
- **Avoid:** generic "AI skin scanner" (no moat), face-rating/looksmaxxing (saturated, reputational and App Review risk), and any skin-condition or mole detection (FDA/MDR, Apple 1.4.1).
- Key success factors: a faceless/UGC TikTok content engine from day 1, transparent pricing (the anti-weekly-trap stance is itself marketing), on-device or delete-after-scan privacy plus BIPA consent, and repeatable scoring.

### Gaps
- Without rank and download data, the "crowded" judgment rests on clone counts, TikTok density and revenue anecdotes rather than a measured market share split.
